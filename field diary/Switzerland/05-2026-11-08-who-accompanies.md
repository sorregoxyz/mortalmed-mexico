# 05. Who accompanies

*2026-11-08. Written the same day, from desk research.*

## What I did
Mapped the organisations that carry out assisted suicide in Switzerland: who they accept, how many deaths, what they cost, where they are. Sources: press (Swissinfo, SRF, Blick, Le Temps, Beobachter), a 2019 overview in Deutsches Ärzteblatt, a legal handbook, and the organisations' own statements as reported. Figures come from different years and should be read as orders of magnitude.

## What I found

| Organisation | Founded | Base | Who it accepts | Scale (latest found) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EXIT (Deutsche Schweiz)** | 1982 | Zurich | Swiss citizens (including abroad) and residents, 18+ | 1,421 deaths in 2025; 195,836 members |
| **EXIT A.D.M.D. Suisse romande** | 1982 | Geneva | Residents only | 502 deaths in 2022; 33,411 members |
| **Dignitas** | 1998 | Forch, canton Zurich | Members from abroad too | 250 deaths in 2023 |
| **Ex International** | 1996 | Bern | Foreigners too | Small |
| **Lifecircle / Eternal Spirit** | 2010s | Basel area | Foreigners too | No new members since 2022 |
| **Pegasos** | 2019 | Basel | Any adult with capacity who pays; no incurable illness required | 200 to 300 a year (RTS estimate) |
| **Athanasios** | 2025 | Basel-Landschaft | Foreigners | Founder's team handled almost 250 foreign cases the year before |

**EXIT**
- The largest by far, and the one that shapes public understanding. Main activity is advance directives; accompaniment is one service among several.
- Accompanies members only: CHF 45 a year or a one-off life membership; accompaniment is free after three years of membership. Its director put the full cost of one death at over CHF 7,000. A waiting period of around 90 days is reported.
- Average age of those accompanied in 2025: 80. In 2024, 76% died at home and 18.5% in care homes; EXIT campaigns for every care home to admit it.
- Members voted in 2021 to keep serving Swiss citizens living abroad.
- It hires on-call infusion specialists: the IV route is part of routine practice.

**EXIT A.D.M.D. Suisse romande**
- A separate association, residents only. Historically more restrictive on psychiatric cases than its German-speaking counterpart. In 2023 it led a referendum in Geneva against repealing the rule that obliges hospitals and care homes to allow assisted suicide.
- Swissinfo reported it charges CHF 100 for an assisted suicide (entry 02).

**Dignitas**
- Founded in 1998 after a split at EXIT's general assembly, by the lawyer Ludwig Minelli (1932 to 2025). Accompanies foreigners, mostly from Germany, the UK and the US, in a house in canton Zurich since 2009. Its position: taking only Swiss people would discriminate against everyone else.

**Lifecircle / Eternal Spirit**
- Twin bodies created by Erika Preisig after she left Dignitas in 2010: Lifecircle for palliative care and prevention, Eternal Spirit for assisted suicide. Deaths took place in an apartment in Basel. No new members since 2022, as Preisig reached retirement age (entry 03).

**Pegasos**
- Founded in Basel in 2019 by Ruedi Habegger, Preisig's brother, after a split. No incurable illness required: adult, capable, able to pay (around CHF 10,000). Reported 200 to 300 deaths a year. Anne Canning died with Pegasos in canton Solothurn, not Basel (entry 02).

**Athanasios and the new organisations**
- Blick (2026) reported that four new organisations offering assisted suicide to foreigners had formed recently. One, Athanasios, was founded in 2025 by a former nurse who had worked for others; his team handled almost 250 foreign cases travelling to Basel-Landschaft the year before. Philip Nitschke's late-2025 guide calls it his new "go-to organisation", after his split with Pegasos.
- The same report: the co-president of the organisation behind the Sarco (entry 02), released after more than two months in custody, later died at 47 with the help of a German organisation.

## What I noticed
**Two systems, not one.** EXIT is a mass membership association for residents, with a long waiting list, advance directives, home deaths and fees that barely cover costs. The foreigners' system is small organisations, fees around CHF 10,000, short stays, rented rooms and houses. The law is the same; the economies are not.

**Basel is the centre of the second system.** Lifecircle, Pegasos, and now Athanasios all cluster in Basel and Basel-Landschaft, with Solothurn next door. EXIT's world is Zurich and the German-speaking cantons at large; the foreigners' world is the Basel corner of the country. That makes Basel the right field site for *Lethal Prescriptions*, and also its most contested one.

**Organisations split like families.** Dignitas split from EXIT; Preisig left Dignitas; her brother founded Pegasos; Nitschke broke with Pegasos and found Athanasios; a nurse who worked for others founded his own. The landscape reproduces itself through schisms, each new organisation slightly more permissive or more international than the last.

**Prices as a legal question.** Article 115 forbids selfish motives (entry 01). CHF 100 at one end, around CHF 10,000 at the other, and a founder who earned over CHF 150,000 from foreign clients, according to Blick. Where altruism ends is being tested in the price list.

**The drug is the constant.** EXIT says it uses only pentobarbital. Across all these organisations the molecule is the same; what varies is who gets to it, how fast, and at what price.

## Open questions
- Why Basel? Proximity to France and Germany and to the airport, cantonal prosecutors' practice, or simply where the Preisig family started?
- Which doctors write prescriptions for the foreigners' organisations, and how many each?
- Which pharmacies supply them, and do the new organisations face the refusals Preisig did?
- How do Basel-Landschaft and Solothurn handle the growing number of foreign deaths (entry 04)?
- Figures to update with each organisation's latest annual report.
