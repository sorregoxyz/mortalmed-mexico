# 03. Who may write it

*2026-11-07. Written the same day, from desk research.*

## What I did
Asked whether there is controversy around the drug itself, beyond Pegasos and the Sarco (entry 02). Read Federal Supreme Court press releases and their coverage (SRF, Swissinfo, Blick), the FMH legal service's commentary, the SAMW/FMH practice guide, and pharmacy trade press.

## What I found
- **Healthy people: Pierre Beck.** In 2017 a retired Geneva doctor, former vice-president of EXIT in French-speaking Switzerland, prescribed pentobarbital to a healthy 86-year-old woman who wanted to die with her seriously ill husband. She took it herself. Convicted in 2019 under the medicines law (suspended 120-day sentence). The Federal Supreme Court overturned that in December 2021, and in March 2024 found he had not broken the narcotics law either. Its reasoning: giving the drug to a healthy person is not a medical or therapeutic question but an ethical and moral one, and cannot be settled by medical or pharmacological knowledge.
- **The profession answers.** The FMH's legal service: no criminal sanction does not mean a doctor can prescribe to a healthy person without professional risk. In May 2022 the FMH adopted the SAMW's revised guidelines, which state that assisted suicide for healthy people is not ethically justifiable. The organisations replied that this ignores the Federal Court and the European Court of Human Rights.
- **The legislature declines.** The Federal Court has said clarification is for the legislature. Parliament rejected framework legislation in 2025 and favoured monitoring instead.
- **Mental illness, and Basel: Erika Preisig.** A family doctor in Liestal (Basel-Landschaft), head of Lifecircle and Eternal Spirit, helped a mentally ill woman die in 2016 without an independent psychiatric assessment of her capacity. Acquitted of intentional homicide; convicted by the Basel-Landschaft criminal court in 2019 for medicine violations (15 months suspended, CHF 20,000 fine). The Federal Supreme Court later acquitted her, ruling that a psychiatric report is not strictly required: she had studied the records, held intensive conversations and spoken to people close to the patient.
- **The drug through the case.** In 2018 pharmacists were reported to be refusing to supply her with pentobarbital. The Federal Court's 2023 decision also describes her obtaining unlabelled doses, storing them, and later giving them to several people.
- **Illicit offers.** While searching I came across spam posts offering "Swiss pentobarbital" without prescription. Same pattern as the Spanish Telegram story (Spain entry 02). Discursive level only: in black-market talk, "Swiss" works as a guarantee of quality.

## What I noticed
Every Swiss controversy is a dispute over the prescription: for whom it may be written (healthy people, mentally ill people), after what checks, by whom, and whether a pharmacist will fill it.

Each institution steps back in turn. The courts say criminal law cannot settle it, because it is an ethical question, not a medical one. The legislature declines to write a law. The medical profession fills the gap with ethics guidelines that bind its members but not the law. And at the end of the chain, a pharmacist can still say no. Switzerland decides who may die through a substance governed by narcotics law, without ever having written a law to say so.

That is the subproject's title made concrete. A *lethal prescription* is not a document; it is the place where four normative orders (criminal law, narcotics law, professional ethics, pharmacy practice) meet and fail to fully agree.

And the field site matters: Basel-Landschaft is where Preisig worked and was prosecuted, and where Pegasos operates (entry 02). One of the most contested places in the country, and a place where the drug has already been stored, refused and stockpiled.

## Open questions
- How do Basel-Landschaft's prosecutors and cantonal pharmacist now handle pentobarbital, after the Preisig cases?
- Do pharmacists in the Basel area have an informal map of who will dispense and who will not?
- How do organisation doctors read the SAMW guideline in practice: as a limit, or as a risk to manage?
- What does "monitoring" mean, if Parliament prefers it to a law? Who would count what?
