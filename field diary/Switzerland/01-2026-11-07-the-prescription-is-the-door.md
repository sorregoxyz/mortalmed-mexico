# 01. The prescription is the door

*2026-11-07. Written the same day, from desk research.*

## What I did
Started where the subproject's title starts: the prescription. Read the Federal Supreme Court's 2006 ruling on pentobarbital without prescription (BGE 133 I 58) and a University of Zurich commentary on it, the 2009 ruling refusing Dignitas its own authorisation, a pharmacy trade article on how the drug is actually supplied, and the main figures (Federal Statistical Office via secondary sources, EXIT's 2025 numbers).

## What I found
- **An old, minimal law.** Assisted suicide has been possible since the 1940s. Article 115 of the Criminal Code only punishes it when done for "selfish motives". There is no dedicated assisted dying law. The person must take the last act themselves; in Belgium, by contrast, a doctor generally administers the dose.
- **The drug is the gatekeeper.** In 2006 the Federal Supreme Court ruled that sodium pentobarbital cannot be supplied to someone who wants to die without a medical prescription, under either narcotics law or medicines law, and that the state has no constitutional duty to make it available without one. It is classed as a narcotic; only doctors and vets can prescribe it.
- **No stockpile.** Dignitas sought its own authorisation to obtain, hold and dispense pentobarbital, including a reserve, on behalf of doctors and patients. Refused in 2009.
- **Compounded, here too.** There are no finished tablets. Pharmacies obtain the substance from specialised suppliers and prepare it, on a narcotics prescription. Any pharmacy may dispense it; none is obliged to. One reported case: after a cantonal procedure, a pharmacist was taken from the supplying pharmacy, and the doctor involved could no longer obtain the drug.
- **Supply side.** Siegfried, founded in Zofingen, is listed among pentobarbital sodium suppliers (see Spain entry 03).
- **Scale.** Resident assisted suicides rose from 63 in 1999 to almost 1,600 in 2022 (2.1% of deaths). 1,729 in 2023 (Federal Statistical Office, via secondary sources). EXIT alone: 1,421 in 2025 in German- and Italian-speaking Switzerland, up from 1,235; 326 of them (23%) elderly people with multiple conditions, up from 205.
- **Uncounted.** Foreign visitors do not appear in these figures. Parliament rejected monitoring assisted-suicide tourism; the Statistical Office says it is considering producing those numbers anyway.

## What I noticed
Spain has a long law and almost no pentobarbital (Spain entry 04). Switzerland has almost no law and is built entirely around pentobarbital. Where the legislature stayed silent, the narcotics regime did the governing. The prescription became the door, and the doctor who writes it became the doorkeeper. That is what *Lethal Prescriptions* is about.

The shared detail across the two countries: the human dose is handmade. In Barcelona one hospital pharmacy compounds every oral dose (Spain entry 03); in Switzerland any willing pharmacy can. In both, a pharmacist sits between the prescription and the glass, and in both the pharmacist can say no. That could be the bridge between the two diaries.

Also worth keeping: the official death statistics only count residents. The people who travel to die in Switzerland are, statistically, nowhere.

## Open questions
- Who writes the prescriptions? Organisation-affiliated doctors, family doctors, a small circle?
- Which pharmacies dispense, and are they known within the organisations? How does a pharmacy decide?
- Where does the raw substance come from, and does Siegfried's listing mean Swiss production?
- How is an unused dose handled when someone changes their mind or dies before the date?
