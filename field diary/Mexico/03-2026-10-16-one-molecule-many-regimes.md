# 03. One molecule, many regimes

*2026-10-16. Reconstructed on 2026-10-20 from working notes and conversations.*

## What I did
Wrote out the comparative puzzle: one molecule, three regimes of death. Switzerland organises it as assisted dying, the United States as punishment, Mexico through markets, veterinary medicine and transnational circulation.

## What I noticed
Mexico matters less as "the place where it is sold" than as the place where different realities of the drug meet. A veterinary product is classified, sold and prescribed within one set of relations; transnational assisted-dying narratives place the same substance in another. These may be different practical realities, not different perspectives on one thing (Mol). So the question changes from *what does pentobarbital mean to different people?* to *what relations make it the object it becomes, and how do these realities coexist or clash?*

I sketched five overlapping "lives": molecular, veterinary, regulatory, transnational, contested. A biography without beginning or end (Appadurai, Kopytoff, Marcus, Law).

## A note to myself
It is odd, and productive, to write about the *lives* of a substance whose career is ending them. Keep the irony; it may become a title.

## Open questions
- Is the molecular life the "real" drug underneath the others? It should not be. The molecule is enacted too, in assays, pharmacopoeias and autopsies.

## Traces
- Mol, *The Body Multiple* (2002); Appadurai (ed.) 1986; Law 2004
