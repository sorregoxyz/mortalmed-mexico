# 02. What the vultures eat

*2026-11-05. Written up on 2026-11-07 from working notes.*

## What I did
Followed the drug past the moment of death. Read the AEMPS safety notes on euthanised carcasses (December 2023 and February 2026), the IREC-led study on barbiturate intoxication in Spanish wildlife, and press reports from Navarra. Then sketched where a Spanish fieldsite might stand.

## What I found
- **The drug outlives its target.** Pentobarbital is persistent in dead bodies. Every product leaflet warns that carcasses must not be eaten by other animals. One documented case involved dogs poisoned by the remains of a horse euthanised two years earlier.
- **Vultures carry the evidence.** A study of Spanish cases from 2004 to 2020 found pentobarbital in 93.3% of barbiturate intoxications. Avian scavengers were the most affected, griffon vultures above all (28 cases), and two thirds of the vulture cases were in Navarra. At least two mass events were linked to euthanised livestock.
- **A bearded vulture**, a species with its own recovery programme, was found electrocuted with pentobarbital in its stomach and liver. The authors suggest the drug may have impaired its balance and reflexes before it hit the cables.
- **Baztán, March 2024.** Navarra's regional police found six dead griffon vultures around cattle carcasses in a protected feeding zone for scavenger species (ZPAEN). Both birds and carcasses tested positive. A criminal investigation was opened.
- **February 2026.** AEMPS reissued its warning after an increase in reported cases, mostly in Asturias and Navarra, with similar cases in Germany.
- **The contested life.** In June 2026, El Español reported a Telegram contact offering to bring a lethal substance into Spain from another European country, with no prescription or pharmacy. The Ministry answered that the legal route runs through the public health system. Keep this at the discursive level, as with Mexico.

## What I noticed
Spain has built infrastructure to feed vultures: feeding zones where livestock carcasses are left on purpose, part conservation, part waste disposal. A euthanised cow placed there turns care for scavengers into a delivery system for the drug. The same institutions that protect the birds poison them, without anyone intending it.

This is the afterlife of a licensed death. In Mexico (entry 14) I noted that the expensive, controlled death goes to valuable animals, and that their carcasses cannot be rendered. Here the question has an answer: some of them go to the vultures.

## Where a fieldsite might stand
Two kinds of site, following the molecule from clinic to carcass:
- **An urban hub, likely Barcelona.** Assisted dying under the LORE, hospital pharmacies that compound the oral solution, the veterinary industry around Sant Just Desvern and Terrassa. Also where I already live and have access.
- **A rural, environmental site: Navarra or Asturias.** Feeding zones, livestock carcasses, wildlife toxicology, regional police. Navarra has the deeper record; Asturias appears in the latest alert. Probably one as main site, the other as comparison.

A hypothesis to offer, not a plan. The shape of any Spanish fieldsite is the PI's call.

## Open questions
- Who decides which carcasses go to feeding zones, and who checks how the animal died?
- Do farmers and vets in Navarra know about the warnings? What changed after Baztán?
- Is there a link between the human and veterinary supply in Spain, or are they two separate worlds that only meet in this map?
