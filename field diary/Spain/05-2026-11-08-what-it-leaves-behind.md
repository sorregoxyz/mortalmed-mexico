# 05. What it leaves behind

*2026-11-08. A framing entry and a working plan. Written after entries 01 to 04 and the first Swiss entries.*

## The question
Entries 01 to 04 kept running into the same wall: in Spain, pentobarbital is almost absent from human assisted dying. Instead of treating that as a weakness, this entry turns the case around. A more-than-human Spain, built around animals, carcasses and the making of the drug, that still speaks to human death.

## Spain in the series
Each MORTALMED site shows one life of the drug:

| Site | The drug's life | The death it makes |
|---|---|---|
| Mexico | Circulation, the market | Bought death |
| United States | Punishment | Imposed death |
| Switzerland | Prescription, right | Chosen death |
| Spain | Afterlife, residue | Death that does not stop |

Mexico sells it, the United States punishes with it, Switzerland prescribes it. Spain lives with what it leaves behind.

The other three sites end at the moment of death. Spain begins there: the drug keeps acting in carcasses, crosses species through scavengers, and outlives its purpose by years (entry 02). It is also the drug's ordinary life. Most pentobarbital kills animals, not people; the other sites are human exceptions, Spain is the routine.

**The inversion.** Spain built a human right to die and kept pentobarbital mostly out of it (entries 01, 04). The same molecule saturates animal death, from the clinic to the cattle carcass. Humans die without it; animals die with it; and what it leaves behind poisons the animals that eat the dead.

**The grant's terms, extended.**
- *Pharmakon*, literal and multispecies: mercy for the euthanised animal, poison for the vulture.
- *Necro-socialities*: feeding zones, rendering plants, shelters, hospital pharmacies. Spaces where death is managed, and where it leaks.
- *Inequality*: which deaths are counted and governed, human (regional access, dying while waiting) and animal (which carcasses are controlled, which poisonings are registered). Differential grievability, as in Tarde's *Insignificant Others*.

**How it still speaks to human death.** Spain wrote two laws on permitted death in two years. The LORE (2021) defines when a person may be helped to die. Law 7/2023 on animal welfare, in force since 29 September 2023, bans killing companion animals for space or economic reasons and allows euthanasia only by a vet, when the animal's welfare is compromised. Two "good deaths", legislated side by side, one with the drug at its margins and one with the drug at its centre.

## Four national controversies
The plan follows the controversies, not just the sites. Four, each with its place:

1. **The right to die in court (urban).** Judicialisation of the LORE: Noelia and Francesc in Barcelona, the Supreme Court's May 2026 ruling giving close relatives standing, one in three applicants dying while waiting, regional inequality (entry 04).
2. **"Sacrificio cero" (urban).** Law 7/2023: the end of killing healthy shelter animals (the director general for animal rights put it at around 100,000 a year), euthanasia only on veterinary judgement, the vets' professional body negotiating how each euthanasia must be justified, hunting dogs left out after a political fight. Where pentobarbital is used, and who decides when.
3. **Carcasses for scavengers (rural).** After the BSE crisis, carcasses could no longer be left in the field, and scavengers lost food; conservationists (SEO/BirdLife) lobbied the EU to allow the old practice again. Royal Decree 1632/2011 lets regions delimit feeding zones for scavenger species; in 2022 and 2023 at least ten regions authorised extensive farms to leave carcasses without removal. A euthanised cow in one of those zones is the Baztán case (entry 02). Conservation policy and veterinary pharmacology collide on the same body.
4. **Illicit circulation (discursive).** The Telegram market reported in June 2026 (entry 02). Followed only through press and public discourse.

Background, kept separate: wild boar culling during the ASF outbreak in Catalonia and wolf culling in Asturias. Not pentobarbital, but the same governance of killing and carcasses I already know from fieldwork.

## A four-year plan
Assumes a start in January 2027 and Barcelona as base. Alternates between rural and urban, with rural fieldwork in the season when livestock is on mountain pastures (to verify for the chosen site).

**Year 1 (2027): Ground and corpus. Barcelona base.**
- Ethics approvals (Glasgow and Spain).
- Documentary corpus: LORE reports, AEMPS safety notes, regional feeding-zone decrees and MITECO reports, court rulings, Law 7/2023 and its veterinary protocols.
- Transparency requests: oral procedures, compounded doses prepared and unused, scavenger poisonings by region.
- Scoping conversations: DMD Catalunya, hospital pharmacists, the Barcelona vets' college, shelter vets, IREC wildlife toxicologists.
- Summer scoping trip to choose between Navarra and Asturias.
- Spain layer of the atlas, version 1.

**Year 2 (2028): Carcass. Main rural fieldwork, roughly April to October.**
- Farmers with feeding-zone authorisations, rural vets, environmental agents and regional police, rendering services, wildlife recovery centres.
- Following the toxicology trail: from a dead bird to a lab result to a safety note.
- Winter in Barcelona: first paper (working title from entry 02, *What the vultures eat*).

**Year 3 (2029): Compound. Main urban fieldwork in Barcelona.**
- Hospital pharmacy compounding and the oral route, with care and only with approvals.
- LORE in practice: commission, DMD, court documents.
- Clinics and shelters under "sacrificio cero": who decides an animal's death, with what.
- The Vallès corridor as a route, from molecule to vial (entry 03).
- Short rural return. Second paper (working title *Two good deaths*: the LORE and Law 7/2023).

**Year 4 (2030): Leave behind. Writing and atlas.**
- Third paper on the inversion: a right to die without the drug.
- Atlas complete, Spain held side by side with the other sites.
- Public output: an exhibition of specimens (specimenography), traces of the drug's afterlife.
- If Switzerland is also part of my work, this is where the comparison is written.

## Risks
- **Sensitivity in the field.** Baztán led to a criminal investigation; farmers may hear "pentobarbital" as an accusation. Enter through conservation and carcass management, not through the poisoning.
- **Human death.** The LORE side needs the most careful ethics design; documents first, people later.
- **Corporate access.** The production corridor may stay documentary.
- **Positioning.** This is a hypothesis, not a proposal. The shape of any Spanish fieldsite is the PI's call, and depends on the ERC.
- **Overreach.** Four controversies in four years is a lot. If one has to go, it is the illicit market.
