# 01. Compounded

*2026-11-05. Written up on 2026-11-07 from working notes.*

## What I did
Took the PI's phrase "compounded pentobarbital" literally and went looking for it in Spanish regulation. Read the Ministry of Health's recommendations on medicines for the assisted dying procedure under the LORE (Ley Orgánica 3/2021), a regional prescription form from Cantabria, the 2024 national annual report, and the authorised veterinary products.

## What I found
- **Two modalities, one marginal.** The LORE allows administration by the care team (mostly intravenous) or self-administration. The oral route is rare: in 2023 only 18 people chose it.
- **Pentobarbital is the drug of choice for the oral route, and it is compounded.** It does not arrive as a finished human medicine. Hospital pharmacies prepare it as a *fórmula magistral*, an oral solution that keeps for a month at room temperature and must not be refrigerated. Secobarbital is the alternative, but its production is very limited. If both run short, the protocol sends the procedure back to the intravenous route.
- **Compounding has a geography.** In Cantabria it is centralised in the pharmacy of Hospital Universitario Marqués de Valdecilla, and two other hospitals must request it in advance.
- **Scale.** 426 people received assisted dying in Spain in 2024, from 905 new requests. 63 of them (14.79%) donated organs afterwards.
- **Pharmacists.** A 2023 survey of hospital pharmacists found 81% knew the law and 63% would not declare themselves conscientious objectors.
- **The veterinary life is industrial.** Several pentobarbital products are authorised for animal euthanasia. One, Eutanax, has its authorisation holder in Sant Just Desvern (Fatro Ibérica) and its batch release in Terrassa (Labiana Life Sciences), both in the Barcelona area.

## What I noticed
In Spain the same molecule exists in two industrial states. For animals it is a finished, registered, labelled product. For humans it is made by hand, dose by dose, in a hospital pharmacy, for a route that almost nobody chooses. The human version is the craft version. That inverts what I would have expected.

The fallback clause is telling. If pentobarbital cannot be had, the law does not stop; the death changes route. The drug is preferred, not necessary. What does it hold that the intravenous protocol does not? Probably the act itself: the oral route is the only one where the person takes it with their own hand.

## Open questions
- Where does the raw pentobarbital that hospital pharmacies compound come from? Same supply chains as the veterinary industry, or separate ones?
- How do other regions organise compounding? Centralised like Cantabria, or hospital by hospital?
- Why is the oral route so marginal in Spain when it is the norm in Switzerland? Clinical preference, the design of the protocol, or something about who is allowed to hold the drug?
- Organ donation after assisted dying: a body that leaves the procedure and re-enters circulation. Does it only happen after the intravenous route?
