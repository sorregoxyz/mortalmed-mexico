# 16. Expanding the map: the Ontological Atlas of Pentobarbital

Since this project follows the multiple lives of pentobarbital in México, I think a good way to deal with, organize, and translate these ontologies is to produce an ontological atlas of pentobarbital. I don't have an idea yet about its shape, but it could be a sort of cabinet, a taxonomic structure organized by proximity and relation, or a schematic arrangement similar to the Porous Containment and Building the Specimen maps, just with a different rationality.

## What is this Atlas?
The Ontological Atlas of Pentobarbital is both a conceptual framing and a working research instrument for the Mexico subproject: rather than treating the drug as a single stable object moving through different contexts, it follows Mol’s and Law’s sense of multiplicity and Appadurai and Kopytoff’s cultural biography of things to trace pentobarbital as a set of overlapping “lives,” molecular, veterinary, regulatory, transnational, and contested, each a distinct entry point into how the substance is known, handled, and governed. 

## How does it work?
Rather than resolving these lives into one linear account, the atlas holds them side by side, in the spirit of Marcus’s multi-sited ethnography, so that the fragmentation of the object across pharmacy, clinic, law, and border becomes the finding itself rather than a problem to be narrated away. In practice, the atlas is already underway as a living, versioned artifact, not just a proposal: it extends directly from the cartography-of-controversies method developed for Porous Containment, and its current seed (v0.1.0) maps the actors and registers around the drug’s Mexico circulation as a navigable instrument that grows alongside the fieldwork, rather than a map produced only at the end of it.

## The Atlas vs the Map
Although the atlas and the map are related, they are doing different analytic work. The map of actors I am already building is actor-centric: it asks who and what surrounds pentobarbital, institutions, laws, papers, discourses, and plots the relations between them. That’s the Porous Containment logic carried over directly: a controversy is made visible by mapping its participants.

The atlas is object-centric: it asks what pentobarbital itself is, in different worlds, at the same time. Molecular life, veterinary life, regulatory life, transnational life, contested life aren’t actors, they’re modes of being the same molecule inhabits simultaneously, which is exactly Mol’s and Law’s point about multiplicity and exactly what “pharmakon” in this project is gesturing at, one substance, several concurrent identities.
