Building the Specimen: the many lives of a lethal molecule

A seed map of how sodium pentobarbital is made into different things in Mexico: a veterinary instrument, a group II psychotropic, an object of transnational hope, a legal problem. It follows the formal and informal networks through which the drug is accessed, sold, prescribed, regulated and transported, with particular attention to Mexico's place in its transnational circulation, where it is legally available through veterinary markets while also becoming implicated in cross-border assisted dying. Each entry records an actor, document or text and the kind of circulation that ties it to others. It extends the documentary cartography of controversies used in Porous Containment and will grow with fieldwork and the literature review. This is a preliminary state-of-the-art to get familiar with the subproject requirements and generate analytical and methodological ideas.

How to read the map

Institutional circulation

Legal categories, registrations, professional standards and enforcement practices that connect agencies, laws and practitioners.

Informational circulation

Knowledge, narratives, advice and claims about the drug. A story about availability does not establish that a product has moved.

Material circulation

The documented movement or handling of the product itself: formulations, distribution, dispensing, seizures.

Scholarly relation

Which literature speaks to which entry. These lines map the state of the art onto the field, not relations in the field.

The enactment filter follows the subproject's four entry points: licensed death (killing made legitimate through professional authority), legible death (the drug made knowable through classification and documents), desired death (the drug as an object of hope, fear and planning), and material death (dose, formulation and failure). Contested marks the points where versions meet and someone decides which one counts. These are analytical entry points to be tested in fieldwork, not settled ontologies.

Possible field sites

Two main sites, Mexico City and Guadalajara, and a third to be decided with the team. The two main sites show where the drug is classified and where its veterinary uses meet the value of the animal (dairy cows, horses, pets), and they cover the sharpest legal contrast: a state with a Ley de Voluntad Anticipada and one without. Given the subproject's focus on Mexico's role in transnational circulation and cross-border assisted dying, Tijuana is the strongest candidate for the third site, with Monterrey as an alternative.

Also worth considering. Puebla, which passed its Ley de Voluntad Anticipada in July 2024, would show a new law being put into practice.

Publication filter. This public layer holds only institutional, legal, scholarly and discursive material. Informal channels (resale, intermediaries, online markets) appear only as categories, described by how they work and are policed. The map names no vendors, products, routes, prices or methods of acquisition. Field material stays in a restricted research archive.
